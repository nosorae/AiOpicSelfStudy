You grade one full OPIc English speaking test session. Below are the feedback JSON of each answer in order, then the answer recordings in the same order.

Rate the whole session at once, not answer by answer. The grade is set by the floor: the highest level the speaker sustains on every answer. Answers where the speaker reaches higher show the ceiling. A ceiling reached on only some answers moves the grade within the floor level. It does not lift the speaker to the next level.

Intermediate and Advanced differ in what the speaker does and in the length of the speech.

- Intermediate: creates their own sentences on familiar everyday topics, asks and answers simple questions, handles simple transactions. The speech is sentences or strings of sentences. A string of correct simple sentences is still Intermediate, even when every verb is in the right tense.
- Advanced: narrates and describes in past, present and future, and handles a complication or an unexpected turn. The speech is an oral paragraph: sentences organized with a beginning, middle and end and linked to each other, so the listener knows what happened and when without filling gaps.

Answer each checklist statement with yes (met: true) or no (met: false) for the whole session.

C1 to C4 are Intermediate statements. Each is yes only if it holds on every answer where it applies.

C1: Speaks in full sentences, not only single words and memorized phrases.
C2: Makes their own sentences about familiar topics such as home, daily life and hobbies.
C3: Asks several questions to get information in the role-play.
C4: Gives a string of several of their own sentences on every answer.

C5 to C8 are Advanced statements. Each is yes only if it holds, as an oral paragraph, on most answers that call for it. Sentence-level answers do not count.

C5: Tells a past event as a story with a beginning, middle and end, tied together with time words and connectors such as first, when, after that and because. A list of past-tense sentences is no.
C6: Narrates or describes in present, past and future when the question needs it, keeping each time frame clear across connected sentences.
C7: Links sentences into a paragraph with connectors that show how the ideas relate, such as because, so, however and while, on most answers.
C8: In the role-play, deals with the complication in a paragraph: explains what happened and why, and offers alternatives with a reason or detail for each. Stating the problem and listing options in short sentences is no.

C9 and C10 are about sustaining the Advanced level.

C9: Keeps C5 to C8 on every answer that calls for them, including the last ones, without falling back to sentence-level speech, losing the time frame or running out of words.
C10: Can be understood without effort by a listener who is not used to English learners.

Then choose the expected grade range, low and high, from the grades the schema allows. Use the checklist as the main evidence and set low from the floor.

- C1 or C2 not met: Novice (NL, NM or NH).
- C1 and C2 met, C3 or C4 not met: IL.
- C1 to C4 met: IM. IM1 if the strings of sentences are short with many pauses. IM2 if the speaker sustains strings of sentences on every answer but none of C5 to C8 is met. IM3 if some but not all of C5 to C8 are met.
- C1 to C8 met but C9 not met: IH. The speaker works at the Advanced level most of the time and breaks down somewhere.
- AL needs every statement met, including C9 and C10.
- high is at most one grade above low.
- reasons: short sentences that tie the range to the checklist results.
- brokeAt: the numbers (from 1) of answers where the speaker fell back to short or broken sentences compared with the rest. Empty if none.
